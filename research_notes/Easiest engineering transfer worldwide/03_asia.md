# Asia Engineering Transfer Options (Malaysia first; India, Indonesia, others) for an NGU CCAS student with mostly D grades and CGPA ~1.8–1.9

Research date: 10 October 2026. Researcher notes for the report writer.

Method note (important for confidence): Web search worked, but direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) failed for almost every target domain (DNS/proxy errors on mqa.gov.my, eac.org.my, *.edu.my, sharda.ac.in, lancaster.ac.uk). Most findings below therefore come from search-engine extracts of the cited official documents, not from reading the full documents. Every item is cited to the page the extract came from. Anything numeric that the writer wants to state as fact should be marked "verify against the full document". Fees come mostly from third-party aggregators because no official fee schedule could be opened.

Labels used for estimates: **Supported by published policy**, **Preliminary estimate**, **Unknown – requires written confirmation**.

## Q1. Malaysia national rules: MQA credit transfer and EAC/BEM limits (minimum grade, maximum %, foreign credits)

### Takeaway
Every Malaysian rule found sets the **minimum transfer grade at C (grade point 2.00) or higher**. Some institutions require C+, and some require B for anything above 30%. **No source says a D can transfer for credit** in Malaysia, and one private-institution policy explicitly excludes C-, D+, D and F. For EAC-accredited B.Eng programmes, lateral Bachelor-to-Bachelor transfer is capped at **50% of total programme credits**. Foreign credit is allowed only with about 80% syllabus match, similar credit value and an accredited source programme. For a mostly-D transcript, Malaysia's structural problem is the grade floor, not the percentage cap.

### Cited Findings
**MQA / Ministry-level rules**
- An MQA-linked policy summary of the amended credit transfer policy (horizontal transfer, including from foreign universities) requires a **minimum grade of C** in the prior course, a similar credit value and **at least 80% curriculum equivalence**. The article is old (about 2015), so verify the current version. — [StudyMalaysia: MQA amends credit transfer policy](https://studymalaysia.com/education/top-stories/mqa-amends-credit-transfer-policy-to-allow-malaysian-students-overseas-to-study-degree-programmes-locally)
- In horizontal transfer, the receiving HEI carries out "subject mapping". Conditions are a minimum grade C, at least 80% curriculum equivalence, similar credit value and accredited programmes only. — search extract citing the same StudyMalaysia article and [3D Universal guide (secondary)](https://3d-universal.com/en/blogs/how-to-transfer-credits-to-malaysian-universities.html)
- MQA/KPM policy review meeting Bil. 3/2019 (10 Dec 2019) raised the vertical (Diploma to Bachelor) cap from 30% to **50%**. The combined 60% cap (with APEL, MOOC and micro-credentials) was kept. Universities apply grade tiers: **C for 1–30%, B for 31–50%**. — [UKM Garis Panduan Pemindahan Kredit Menegak 18/10/2023](https://www.ukm.my/akademik/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/GARIS-PANDUAN-MENEGAK-18102023.pdf); [UMK Sisipan Pindaan Peraturan Akademik 5.4.1.5](https://fsb.umk.edu.my/download/download/Sisipan_Pindaan_Peraturan_Akademik_Peraturan_5.4.1.5.pdf)
- MQA's MOOC credit transfer guideline caps MOOC credit at 30% of graduating credits. This covers MOOCs only, not university-to-university transfer. — [MQA Guidelines on Credit Transfer for MOOC (2017)](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/qad/garispanduan/2017/MOOC.pdf)
- No current (2023–2026) MQA horizontal-transfer policy document was found. The most recent official change found is the 2019 decision above. — Gap noted by search; see Gaps.

**EAC (Board of Engineers Malaysia) rules for accredited B.Eng**
- EAC Engineering Programme Accreditation Standard 2020 sets a maximum of **50% of total credit hours for lateral credit transfer/credit exemption (Bachelor to Bachelor)** and a **maximum 30% credit exemption for Diploma to Degree**. Programmes must have documented credit transfer policies. This comes from a search extract of a mirrored copy and was not read in full. — [EAC Standard 2020 (eac.org.my PDF)](https://eac.org.my/v2/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/EAC-Standard-2020.pdf); [mirror (anyflip)](https://anyflip.com/lrokg/avqv/basic/51-100)
- UTAR states that engineering transfers are capped at 50% of the graduation requirement "as per credit transfer guidelines set by the Engineering Accreditation Council (EAC)". — [UTAR Credit Transfer](https://admission.utar.edu.my/Credit-Transfer.php)
- UTP: "For Engineering program, or program accredited by EAC: Credit Transfer between accredited/recognised programmes of same level, i.e. from Bachelor to Bachelor degree – maximum Credit Transfer of 50% of the total programme credits." The minimum grade is **C / 60%**. — [UTP REX – Credit Transfer FAQ](https://utp.microsoftcrmportals.com/knowledgebase/article/KA-01339/en-us)
- EAC is BEM's delegated body and the only recognised accreditor of engineering degree programmes in Malaysia. Malaysia is a Washington Accord signatory through BEM. — [BEM – EAC](https://bem.org.my/Landing/acc); [EAC list of accredited programmes](https://eac.org.my/list-of-accredited-engineering-programme-malaysia/)
- No explicit EAC "minimum residency" clause, other than the 50% cap, was found. — search result summary for EAC Standard 2020 (see Gaps)

**Institution-level evidence on minimum grade (shows the national pattern)**
- MMU (private): "minimum grade of C or its equivalent (satisfactory performance or a pass)". — [MMU Credit Transfer Policy, Senate-approved, 03/03/2022](https://creative.mmu.edu.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/i649_Credit-Transfer-Policy_03032022-1.pdf)
- SIUC (Malaysian private institution): "credit transfer is not allowed for courses with grades below C such as C-, D+, D & F". — [SIUC Credit Transfer, Course Exemption & Student Transfer Policy (2025)](https://siuc.edu.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Policy-Credit-Transfer-Course-Exemption-and-Student-Transfer.pdf)
- Sunway University: 30% transfer allowed with grade C, and 31–50% only with grade B. — [Sunway University Academic Regulations 2024(ii), hosted by Lancaster](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/media/lancaster-university/content-assets/documents/student-based-services/asq/collaborative-provision/Sunway-assess-regs.pdf)
- IMU: minimum grade C or GPA 2.0, "based on MQA guidelines" (updated 12 Sep 2024). — [IMU Credit Transfer Policy](https://www.imu.edu.my/wp-content/uploads/programme/adm-credit-transfer-policy.pdf)
- UniSHAMS cites MQA/KPT as the basis for a C minimum and a 30% limit. — [UniSHAMS Credit Transfer Guidelines](https://unishams.edu.my/index.php/component/content/article/credit-transfer-guideline.html?catid=8)
- Universiti Malaya: floor of "50 marks or grade C or grade point 2.00" for Bachelor programmes. — [UM Guidelines on Transfer of Credit and Course Exemption](https://fsktm.um.edu.my/fsktm/doc/undergraduate/TK-2UNIVERSITI%20MALAYA%20GUIDELINES%20ON%20THE%20MANAGEMENT%20OF%20APPLICATION%20FOR%20TRANSFER%20OF%20CREDIT%20AND%20COURSE%20EXEMPTION.pdf)
- UTHM: minimum **C+**, and faculties may set a higher minimum. UTHM also has a "credit transfer without grade" (credit exemption) concept. — [UTHM Credit Transfer Guidelines](https://amo.uthm.edu.my/images/UPPP/Credit_Transfer_Guidelines.pdf)
- UTM Civil Engineering: "a minimum of a Passed grade as set by the Faculty". The page does not name a letter, is over a decade old and is a public university. — [UTM Civil: Guidelines for Exemption and Transfer of Credit](http://engineering.utm.my/civil/current-students/academics-guidelines/guidelines-for-exemption-and-transfer-of-credit/)
- A general guide notes that professionally accredited programmes such as engineering "may impose stricter criteria". — [Monash Malaysia: How to transfer university credit](https://www.monash.edu.my/news-and-events/trending/transfer-university-credit-malaysia)

### Inferences
- **D grades almost certainly do not transfer at any Malaysian EAC-accredited B.Eng.** The pattern is C (2.00) minimum everywhere found, with stricter rules at some institutions (C+, or B above 30%). This rests on many institutional policies citing MQA. It is not 100% proven for every private university, but no counter-example was found.
- The 50% EAC cap is irrelevant for this student. With mostly D grades, the transferable share will be far below 50%.
- The 80% syllabus-match rule is a second filter. NGU CCAS courses (autonomous systems, communications) will map partially onto Malaysian EEE, Computer or Mechatronic curricula. Maths, physics, circuits and programming courses are the most likely matches, but only where the grade is C or better.
- "Credit exemption without grade" (UTHM wording) exists at some universities. Whether any private university would exempt a D course this way is **Unknown – requires written confirmation**. No evidence supports it.

### Gaps
- The current full-text MQA "Policy on Credit Transfer" / "Guidelines to Good Practices" (post-2019 revision, if any) could not be retrieved. Verify at mqa.gov.my (Policies > Credit Transfer) or by emailing MQA.
- The EAC Standard 2020 full text was not read, and whether an EAC Standard 2024 revision exists was not checked. The 50%/30% figures come from search extracts. Verify at eac.org.my, under Standard/Manual.
- Whether MQA rules require the *foreign* source programme to be "accredited", and how an Egyptian private university's programme (NGU) is treated: not confirmed. Ask the target university's credit transfer office.

## Q2. Malaysian candidate institutions: transfer rules, admission flexibility for CGPA < 2.0, costs

### Takeaway
No Malaysian private university was found to publish a transfer-admission route that accepts CGPA < 2.0, or to accept D grades for credit. Admission and transfer are case-by-case ("discretion"), so the realistic Malaysia scenario is **near-restart**: entry to Year 1, possibly with a few C-or-better courses exempted. That means about 3.5–4 years of B.Eng study at roughly RM 100k–137k tuition. On priorities 2–4 (credit acceptance, time, money) Malaysia is weak for this profile. It scores well only on legitimacy (EAC/Washington Accord) and on English-taught, physical-residence study, which matters for the Egyptian syndicate.

## Q3. Malaysia B.Eng vs Bachelor of Engineering Technology (ETAC / Sydney Accord) and Egyptian recognition risk

### Takeaway
Malaysian BEng Tech degrees are accredited by ETAC under the **Sydney Accord**, i.e. for engineering *technologists*, not engineers. That makes Egyptian Engineers Syndicate registration risky. Only Washington Accord B.Eng (EAC) is the safe Malaysian choice. Egyptian equivalence is decided solely by the SCU, which compares level, content and **duration**. Malaysian B.Eng programmes are typically 4 years, while Egyptian B.Sc. Engineering is 5 years including the preparatory year. This duration gap is an unquantified risk.

### Cited Findings
- Malaysia (through BEM) became a full signatory of the **Sydney Accord** (engineering technology, typically Bachelor in Engineering Technology) and the Dublin Accord in June 2018. Sydney Accord qualifications are "substantially equivalent ... for practice as an engineering technologist". — [IPM: Full Signatory of Sydney Accord and Dublin Accord](https://ipm.my/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Full-Signatory-of-Sydney-Accord-and-Dublin-Accord-for-The-Future-Global-Engineering-Workforce.pdf); [IEA Sydney Accord](https://accreditation.org/accreditation-processes/accords/sydney-accord)
- ETAC is BEM's delegated body and the only recognised accreditor for engineering technology bachelor and diploma programmes in Malaysia. — [BEM accreditation page](https://bem.org.my/Landing/acc)
- Egypt's Ministry of Higher Education states the **Supreme Council of Universities (SCU) is the only legal body for equivalence** of degrees from inside and outside Egypt. It warned syndicates against issuing their own university evaluations. Applications go online through the SCU, followed by courier submission, and take about 1.5–2 months. — [Cairo24: equivalence of a Malaysian engineering bachelor and syndicate registration](https://www.cairo24.com/1421057)
- Equivalence compares the foreign qualification with its Egyptian counterpart by "academic level, scientific content, and **duration of study**". — [Edugate UAE (secondary, Arabic)](https://edugateuae.com/ar/blog-detail/%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A)
- The Engineers Syndicate has refused to register holders of SCU equivalence whose underlying qualification breached its rules. In 2023 it said it would not register graduates of engineering institutes lacking quality certification, i.e. the syndicate applies its own registration rules on top of SCU equivalence. — [Youm7, 2023](https://www.youm7.com/6260067); [Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5238167.aspx)
- The SCU equivalence portal is equ.scu.eg (cited in Egyptian press). — [Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/4824434)

### Inferences
- Avoid Malaysian BEng Tech (ETAC/Sydney Accord) if syndicate registration matters. The "technologist" status creates a high risk of non-registration (**Preliminary estimate**; not confirmed by any syndicate ruling found).
- A 4-year Malaysian EAC B.Eng carries an unresolved **duration risk** for SCU equivalence. Ask the SCU and the Egyptian Cultural Bureau in Kuala Lumpur in writing **before enrolling**.

### Gaps
- No SCU list of recognised Malaysian universities, and no syndicate decision on 4-year foreign B.Eng degrees, was found. Verify at equ.scu.eg, the Egyptian Engineers Syndicate registration committee (لجنة القيد), and the Egyptian Cultural Bureau in Malaysia.
- Whether partial study at NGU followed by a foreign degree raises extra SCU scrutiny: unknown.

## Q4. India: lateral entry / transfer for international students into B.Tech

### Takeaway
India's "lateral entry" is a domestic AICTE/state route for **Indian diploma holders** into the 3rd semester. No AICTE rule found provides foreign-transcript transfer into B.Tech. Private universities (Sharda especially) have internal "migration" policies with an equivalence committee. Sharda requires at least **70% syllabus overlap** and that at least 70% of earned credits be transferable, which is unlikely to be met by a mostly-D CCAS transcript. India is a low-confidence option. It is also a weaker option for Egyptian recognition, since AICTE-approved B.Tech degrees are 4 years.

### Cited Findings
- Lateral entry admits **3-year diploma holders** from AICTE-approved or state-recognised institutions directly into the 3rd semester. States run the process (ECET, OCET, etc.), and a typical minimum is about 45% (40% for reserved categories). — [Sunstone: Lateral entry in B.Tech 2026-27](https://sunstone.in/blog/pros-and-cons-of-lateral-entry-in-btech); [CollegeBatch](https://www.collegebatch.com/blog/what-is-btech-lateral-entry/)
- No AICTE rule covering foreign-national transfer into B.Tech 2nd year was found by search. — search summary (see Gaps)
- **Sharda University** (Greater Noida, private): its 2026-27 credit-transfer page says students from other universities can transfer credits to Sharda and graduate with a Sharda degree, paying tuition, accommodation and registration. — [Sharda Credit Transfer 2026-27](https://www.sharda.ac.in/international/credittransfer)
- Sharda SOP on course mapping and credit transfer for migration: the previous syllabus must overlap **at least 70%**, at least 70% of earned credits must be transferable, and 1 credit equals 15 lecture hours. — [Sharda SOP](https://www.sharda.ac.in/attachments/sops/SOP_on_Course_Mapping_Credit_Transfer_for_Migration3.pdf)
- An Equivalence Committee decides cases (Dean Academic Affairs, CoE, Director Admission, Dean/HoD, two VC nominees) and may prescribe bridge courses. — [Sharda Policy for Migration AY 2023-24 onwards](https://www.sharda.ac.in/attachments/downloadcenter_files/Policy_for_Migration_of_Students_Academic_Year_2023-24_onwards.pdf)
- An older Sharda migration policy requires the student to "have passed all courses of their previous semester", and unmapped courses must be passed at Sharda. — [Sharda Policy Migration](https://www.sharda.ac.in/attachments/common_files/Policy-Migration.pdf)
- No minimum grade for transfer was found in Sharda documents (the Ordinances, s.8.7, cover grading). — [Sharda Ordinances amended to July 2024](https://sharda.ac.in/attachments/iqac/Ordinances_of_Sharda_University_amended_up_to_July_2024.pdf)
- **LPU** and **Chandigarh University** "international credit transfer" programmes run **outbound** (2 years in India, then abroad). No inbound transfer route for foreign credits was found. — [LPU blog](https://www.lpu.in/blog/why-b-tech-cse-2-2-with-international-credit-transfer-option-at-lpu-stands-out/); [CU International Transfer Programs](https://www.cuchd.in/international/program-search-criteria.php)
- The Amity transfer policy found belongs to **Amity University UK**, not the Indian campuses. — [Amity UK student transfer policy](https://www.amity.ac.uk/s/policy_student_transfer_v1.pdf)
- Comparator: some Indian private universities set a minimum transfer grade of "C" (e.g. Periyar Maniammai). — [PMU Course Equivalence and Credit Transfer](https://www.pmu.edu/assets-v2/pdf/regulations/Course-Equivalence-and-Credit-Transfer.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Sharda** is the only Indian candidate with a documented inbound transfer mechanism. The 70% overlap / 70% transferable threshold (as extracted) and the "passed all courses" requirement are likely to be problems if any NGU courses were failed or withdrawn. Credit estimate: **Unknown – requires written confirmation**.
- Indian degree recognition with the Egyptian SCU/syndicate is not researched here. It would need the same SCU check.

### Gaps
- AICTE's current Approval Process Handbook provisions for foreign nationals / supernumerary seats and transfer were not retrieved. Verify at aicte-india.org (APH 2026-27).
- Sharda's minimum grade for transferable courses, its fees for international transfer students and its contact were not found. Contact: Sharda International Admissions (global.sharda.ac.in).

## Q5. Indonesia, China, Japan, Korea, Thailand, Philippines, Pakistan, Central Asia: quick screen

### Takeaway
No credible English-taught engineering programme in these countries was found with a published lenient transfer rule accepting D grades. Indonesia's national scale treats D as the lowest pass (1.0), but no evidence was found of an Indonesian university awarding transfer credit for D grades to an incoming international student. All are **dropped** for lack of evidence, with Indonesia as a weak "ask if desired" item.

### Cited Findings
- Indonesia (DIKTI scale): D is the lowest passing grade, with grade point 1.0 on a 4.0 scale. — [OpenEduCat Indonesia grading (secondary)](https://openeducat.org/ar/articles/indonesia-grading-system-ips-ipk-explained/)
- An Indonesian university (UPI) has a "Transfer Kredit" page, but its terms were not retrieved. — [UPI Transfer Kredit](https://pjkr.upi.edu/content/isi?id=27)
- Philippines: inverted grading (1.0 best, 3.0 minimum pass), so a "D" has no direct equivalent. — [OpenEduCat SE Asia grading scale (secondary)](https://openeducat.org/gradebook/southeast-asia/grading-scale/)
- Pakistan comparator: Sarhad University (SUIT) requires grade C or better for transfer. — [SUIT Migration/Transfer Policy](https://suit.edu.pk/transfer)
- China, Japan, Korea, Thailand, Central Asia: no relevant English-taught engineering transfer policies were found in the searches run. — (no source; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- The global norm found in Asia is C-minimum for transfer credit, so no Asian country stands out as better than Malaysia for D grades on the evidence gathered.
- For this student, the realistic Asian route is **re-admission as a new Year-1 student** (based on the secondary certificate) to a low-cost EAC-accredited B.Eng, rather than a credit transfer.

### Gaps
