## What this means for you

1. **Both UK degrees are almost the same programme.** Your NGU years 1–2 line up mainly with their foundation year and Year 1. The realistic outcome at either is skipping the foundation year and possibly Year 1, then studying Years 2 and 3 (about 4 semesters).
2. **The final year is never credited at either university.** The individual project and Level-6 modules must be taken there.
3. **Power engineering is the main gap.** Both EEE degrees teach electric power, machines and power electronics, which CCAS does not appear to emphasise. Expect to learn this from scratch, usually in Year 2. Ask both universities whether a closer route exists, for example Herts BEng Mechanical Engineering & Mechatronics or a Coventry communications- or computer-oriented engineering award.
4. **Coventry's final year is slightly closer to CCAS.** It has Signal Processing and Machine Learning, plus a choice of Control Systems or Communication Systems. Hertfordshire's final year is more hardware-heavy, with Microelectronics/VLSI and Advanced Power Conversion, although it also has Intelligent Systems and Robotics. If you are stronger in communications and AI than in hardware, that leans slightly toward Coventry.
5. **Your D grades matter most in maths, circuits and signals.** Those are the courses most likely to be matched, and each university decides whether a D is good enough. Repeating your weakest maths and circuits courses at NGU to get a C helps both your GPA and your credit.
